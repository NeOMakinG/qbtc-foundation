Bitcoin Quantum Readiness Pledge

We recognize that Bitcoin’s long-term security depends on preparing for cryptographic threats before they become operational emergencies.

A sufficiently capable quantum computer could use Shor’s algorithm to compromise the elliptic-curve cryptography used by Bitcoin’s ECDSA and Schnorr signatures. Once this capability is available to an adversary, exposed public keys and legacy signing systems will be vulnerable before the ecosystem can safely coordinate a response.

We therefore commit to quantum readiness by 31 December 2027.

We commit to

  1. Governance and accountability. Appoint a named senior owner for quantum-readiness strategy and report progress annually.
  2. Cryptographic inventory. Identify and maintain an inventory of systems, products, vendors, wallets, keys, signing workflows, recovery processes and user journeys that depend on elliptic-curve cryptography.
  3. Crypto-agility. Design, procure and operate systems that can support secure migration of signing algorithms, key formats, certificates, hardware and operating procedures without replacing core infrastructure.
  4. Post-quantum implementation. Build and test post-quantum signature capability in relevant systems. Where appropriate, adopt NIST-standardized algorithms such as FIPS 204 ML-DSA and other Bitcoin-compatible protections.
  5. Bitcoin-compatible migration readiness. Support open evaluation of post-quantum designs, including security, privacy, validation cost, block-space impact, hardware requirements, multisignature compatibility, recovery and migration.
  6. Protection of customer and corporate assets. Conduct regular technical tests, incident exercises and independent reviews of quantum-readiness plans.
  7. Ecosystem and supply-chain readiness. Require wallet, HSM, MPC, custody, cloud and software vendors to disclose their quantum-readiness plans.
  8. Testing and assurance. Regularly test our plans through technical exercises and independent security reviews.
  9. Responsible communication. Communicate accurately: preparedness is prudent risk management, not a prediction of an imminent attack. Do not represent a cryptographic scheme as a complete Bitcoin solution before public technical review and ecosystem consensus.
  10. Collaboration. Share non-sensitive lessons, support interoperable standards and collaborate with Bitcoin developers, cryptographers, infrastructure providers, researchers and users.

Our 2027 commitment

By 31 December 2027, we will have documented and tested our quantum-readiness programme; crypto-agile signing infrastructure; evaluated post-quantum implementations; and a practical plan for supporting a safe Bitcoin migration when the ecosystem adopts a suitable post-quantum standard.
